Execution plan · v0.1

Zero to Launch

The spec says what to build. The build guide says how it stays separated. This says what you actually do, in what order, with what money — and where you are allowed to stop.

Phases 6 Kill gates 5 To ten users ~$1,000 · 6–9 months of evenings
01

What "launch" means

Pick one now. Each is a different company, and two of them you are not trying to start.

Launch asYou shipYou becomeVerdict
Personal tool Nothing. You wear it. Someone with very good glasses A legitimate ending. Reached at phase 2.
Open platform Protocol, node, reference build guide, three apps The maintainer of a standard Recommended
Hardware product Assembled glasses, in boxes, with support A hardware company: certification, tooling, RMAs, inventory A different life. Not this project.
Why open platform

Your own architecture doc already reached this conclusion in §24–25: the defensible thing is the device protocol and the node, not the frames. The Pi is replaceable, the sensor is replaceable, the glasses are replaceable — the protocol third parties build against is not.

So: publish the protocol and the node, publish a reference build anyone can assemble from $60 of parts, and let other people make the hardware. You get the ecosystem without becoming a manufacturer, which is the specific way projects like this die.

If you ever do sell assembled hardware, one practical note worth knowing now: build around a pre-certified radio module (the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 is FCC/CE certified) and use its reference antenna. That qualifies you for modular approval and drops radio certification from a five-figure exercise to a much smaller one. Designing your own RF front end reverses that.

02

The riskiest assumption Test this first

It is not technical, and it is testable in two weeks with no hardware.

Everything in the spec is buildable. None of it is research. The real risk is quieter:

  1. Will you actually reach for it? Wearables die of disuse, not of engineering failure. Most people who build one wear it for a fortnight and stop.
  2. Are the answers good enough to be worth asking? "What is that?" against a real street scene is a much harder question than against a clean product photo.
  3. Then, distantly: latency, battery, comfort. All solvable, all measurable, all in the spec.
The order this implies

Do not build glasses to find out whether you want glasses. Put a phone in your shirt pocket with the camera poking out, wear an earbud, and use it exactly as if it were the real thing — for two weeks, in public, in shops, on walks.

It costs nothing and it answers the only question that can kill the project after you have spent six months on it.

03

Six phases, five gates

Every gate has an exit. The cheapest exit comes first, on purpose.

04

Phase −1 · Fake it 2 weeks · $0

No hardware, no node, no code you keep. You are buying information, not building.

The rig

  • Phone in your shirt pocket, camera lens poking over the edge, one earbud in.
  • A shortcut or a tiny app: press → photo + record → send to a hosted multimodal model → speak the answer. An afternoon's work, and you throw it away.
  • Use a hosted model here deliberately. You are testing whether the experience is worth having, not whether you can self-host it.

What to record, every time

Keep a note. One line per use: what you asked, whether the answer was useful, and what you wished it had done instead. Two weeks of that note is worth more than any amount of architecture.

MeasureHowGate
Uses per dayCount them3+ per day, sustained across week 2
Useful rateYour own yes/no per useOver half genuinely useful
What you askedThe noteClusters into 3–5 recurring intents
Week 2 vs week 1Compare countsFlat or up. A drop is the answer.
Gate 1

If usage falls off in week two, glasses will not rescue it. Better hardware makes a useful thing convenient; it does not make an unused thing used. Stop here, having spent a fortnight and nothing else — or take what the note actually says people want and redesign around that.

There is a real chance the note tells you the product is something adjacent: hands-free capture, or memory, or reading signs in another language. That is not failure, it is the cheapest possible pivot.

05

Phase 0 · The node 4 weeks

All of it testable with curl. Do not touch a soldering iron in this phase.

This is the biggest realisation about sequencing: the entire system can be built and measured before any hardware exists. A laptop webcam and a microphone stand in for the glasses perfectly, because the glasses are stateless by design (spec §3.1).

WeekBuildProves
1Schema with person_id, RLS forced, two seeded people, the CI checkIsolation is structural from commit 1
2Capability host: invoke(), person context, audit in the same transactionOne door, and it is the only one
3Pipeline: Whisper, a 4B VLM under MLX, Piper. Pure functions.The models work and fit in memory
4Wire it together, measure end to end, optimise per spec §12The latency budget is real
Gate 2

curl an image and a question, get spoken audio back in under 1.2 seconds, twenty times in a row. Then run two people's requests through a connection pooler simultaneously and confirm neither sees the other's rows.

If latency will not come under about two seconds here, it never will — everything after this adds time, none of it removes any. That is a legitimate stop.

06

Phase 1 · The wearable 4 weeks

Breadboard, then a clip. You are not making frames.

  1. Week 1 — on the bench. XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense, USB power, no battery. Capture a frame, open a WebSocket, ship it, play the reply through a wired transducer. Ugly is fine.
  2. Week 2 — untethered. Add the LiPo and charge circuit. Measure actual draw against the §11 budget, because your firmware will be worse than the model.
  3. Week 3 — on your face. 3D-print a clip-on module for a cheap pair of safety glasses or readers. Expect four or five iterations; each is a few pounds of filament and a night.
  4. Week 4 — wear it. Every day, in public. Fix what annoys you, not what is elegant.
Do not make frames

Injection-mould tooling is five figures and it is the last thing you would ever do, if ever. Buy frames, print a clip. It will look like a prototype. Everyone who matters at this stage knows what a prototype looks like.

Gate 3

Wear it for a full week. Does it survive contact with your actual life — battery lasting the day, no burning temple, no fiddling to make it connect, not embarrassing enough to leave at home? A no here is usually a comfort or battery problem, both of which are fixable. A no because you stopped wanting to is gate 1 arriving late.

07

Phase 2 · Three apps 3 weeks

Built against the same capability API third parties will eventually get. No app store yet.

AppExercisesDone when
What is this?Capture → vision → speech; the whole latency pathUnder 1.2 s from end of speech, on the glasses, twenty times
Remember this.The observation schema and retrievalA question a week later returns the right row from SQL, not from a model's recollection
Add this to my list.Intent routing, capability tokens, identity nodeLands on the right person's list, with the photo, with the internet unplugged

Then add the second person. Per the build guide, this is the checkpoint that matters: it must require zero schema changes, zero new parameters and zero special cases. If it does not, phases 0–2 were built single-tenant and this is the cheapest moment you will ever have to find out.

Gate 4 — and a legitimate ending

Two people, two pairs of glasses, one node, three apps, no code changes between the first person and the second.

You can stop here. You have the thing you wanted, it works, and you own all of it. Everything past this point is about other people, which is a different kind of work and not everyone wants it.

08

Phase 3 · Ten people 8 weeks

The first phase where you find out whether this is a product or a very good hobby.

Who

Ten people who will tell you the truth, not ten people who will be polite. Prefer someone with a reason to want it — a mechanic, a birder, someone with low vision, a warehouse manager — over ten friends who think it is cool. A specific need produces specific feedback.

What you have to build for them that you did not need yourself

  • Pairing that a stranger can do. You have been running adb commands. They will not.
  • An answer to "my node is off." The degradation matrix stops being theoretical.
  • Ten assembled units. Budget a full weekend; hand assembly is slower than you think and the tenth is not much faster than the first.
  • A way to see what broke without asking them to read logs.

What to measure

MeasureMeaningGate

Gate 5

Week-four retention is the honest number. Everybody uses a new gadget in week one. If six of ten are still wearing it in week four, you have something. If two are, you have a demonstration — which is fine, and knowing it now is worth eight weeks.

09

Phase 4 · Ship it 4 weeks

Launch is a publishing exercise, not a manufacturing one.

ArtefactWhat it isWhy it matters
Protocologp/1 schema, frozen, versioned, with a conformance test suiteThe thing other hardware implements. Freeze it or nobody can build against it.
NodeSource, migrations, one-command installThe product. Everything else is a client of it.
Reference buildBOM, wiring, printable STLs, firmware imageSomeone can have working glasses for about $60 and a weekend
Three appsSource, as worked examples of the capability APIDocumentation people can run
Honest READMEWhat works, what does not, battery life, latency, what it cannot doThe single best filter for the users you want

Choose a licence deliberately. Permissive for the protocol — you want it adopted, including by people who will not share back. Copyleft is defensible for the node if you care that hosted forks contribute upstream. These two do not have to match, and for a platform play they usually should not.

The measure of a launch, here

Not stars and not press. Someone you have never met builds the reference hardware and opens an issue about it. That is the moment the protocol stopped being your project and started being a standard, and it is the only signal at this stage that means anything.

What to write, in order

Ten files. Each one is testable before the next exists.

#FileTestable by
1proto/ogp-1.jsonSchema validates a handcrafted handshake. Everything downstream references it, so it goes first.
2node/store/migrations/0001_init.sqlTwo seeded people; B's context cannot read A's rows
3node/store/check-rls.testFails on a table added without person_id or forced RLS
4node/capabilities/host.tsAn invoke() with a forged scope is refused
5node/pipeline/{stt,vision,tts}.tsEach called directly with a fixture file
6node/router/classify.tsTwenty recorded utterances route correctly
7node/gateway/session.tsA websocket client binds and gets a scoped token
8apps/what-is-this.tsEnd to end over curl, timed
9firmware/main.cThe real device replaces curl. Ninth, not first.
10companion/Hotspot, GPS, offline queue with idempotency keys
Why firmware is ninth

The glasses are stateless by design, so a laptop webcam and a curl command are a complete stand-in. Writing firmware first means debugging your architecture through a serial cable at 115200 baud, which is roughly ten times slower than debugging it in a terminal.

The one that actually gets people

Not any single failure — it is spending four months on the node and the firmware before discovering you only reach for it twice a week. Every hour of phase −1 is insurance against the most expensive version of being wrong, and it is the phase people skip because it does not feel like building.

14

Monday morning

The first week, concretely.

  1. Mon. Write the phone rig. A shortcut or a fifty-line app: button → photo + audio → hosted multimodal model → speak the answer. Ship it badly; it gets deleted in two weeks.
  2. Tue. Start a plain text file, one line per use: what you asked, was it useful, what you wished it did. Carry the phone in your shirt pocket from today.
  3. Wed. Use it in a shop. This is the real test — public, hands busy, moderate noise.
  4. Thu. Order nothing. It is deliberate. Gate 1 is nine days away.
  5. Fri. Read your own note. What are the three things you keep asking for?
  6. Weekend. Wear it out properly. Not around the house — around other people, which is where you find out whether you are comfortable using it.

In parallel, if you want something to build while the fortnight runs: write proto/ogp-1.json. It is pure specification, it needs no hardware and no models, and it is the one artefact that survives whatever phase −1 tells you — because if the answer is "the product is memory, not identification", the protocol barely changes.

Then, on day fifteen

Count the note. Three or more uses a day in week two, still climbing or flat: buy the node and start phase 0. Fewer: you have learned the most valuable thing in this entire document for the price of two weeks and no money, and either ending is a good one.
