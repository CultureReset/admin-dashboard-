Build plan · iOS-shaped shell on Linux

Cupertino Pi

An iPhone face over a Raspberry Pi, fed by your home node. What to build, in what order, and which open source does the parts you should not write yourself.

Target Raspberry Pi 5 · 8 GB Prototype Phase 1–2, live Effort ~4 months, evenings

Three systems, not one

Splitting these is the most useful decision in the whole project.

What you described gets conflated constantly, and conflating it is why these projects stall. It is three independent pieces with three different risk profiles.

Layer 1 The shell — lock screen, home grid, dock, Control Center, notifications, app switcher, Settings. The part that has to look and feel like iOS. Highest design cost, lowest technical risk.
Layer 2 The compositor — puts other programs' windows inside your shell, so an app opens by zooming out of its icon. Without this you have a pretty launcher, not an OS. Highest technical risk.
Layer 3 The transports — how apps actually arrive. Three of them, each solving a different problem. Lowest risk: all three are off-the-shelf.
waypipeLinux apps from the home node, each as its own Wayland surface.
Waydroid / redroidAndroid apps, locally in a container or streamed from the cloud.
Sunshine → MoonlightThe whole desktop, hardware encoded, for heavy apps.

All three ride one flat WireGuard or Tailscale network. Set that up first — before any UI work — so the Pi, the home node and the cloud Android instance can address each other directly. Every later decision gets easier once the network is boring.

Prototype in web, ship in QML. You will get pixel-accurate in days on your laptop, where the feedback loop is instant and nothing is cross-compiled. The linked prototype is that step, already done.

Then port the shell to QML, because it is the only option where the shell is the compositor is a solved, shipping pattern — Plasma Mobile and Ubuntu Touch's Lomiri both work exactly this way, and both are open source you can read rather than invent.

Order of work

Each phase is usable on its own and needs the one before it.

1

Static shell, on your laptop

Home grid, pagination, dock, status bar, squircle icons, wallpaper, spring physics. No Pi, no cross-compiling, no compositor. Iterate where reload is instant.

Done when you can hand someone your phone with the page open and they hesitate before deciding it isn't a real iPhone.

2

Gestures and system surfaces

Swipe-up-to-home, app switcher, Control Center, Notification Center, lock screen. Every one of them interruptible — you can catch a mid-flight animation with your finger and drag it back.

Done when no transition can be caught in a state it cannot recover from, and a fast gesture never leaves the shell in the wrong screen.

3

Settings as a schema

Do not hand-build hundreds of panes. Build one iOS-style list renderer driven by a declarative tree — pane, sections, rows, control type, backend binding — then bind rows to real services over D-Bus: NetworkManager for Wi-Fi, BlueZ for Bluetooth, PipeWire for audio, systemd-timedated for time.

Done when adding a settings pane is a data change, and four rows (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, volume, brightness) move real hardware.

4

Become a compositor

The hard phase. Host one real application surface and nail the zoom-from-icon open and close. Read Plasma Mobile's window management before writing your own — the lifecycle edge cases (a surface that dies mid-animation, an app that refuses to close) are where the weeks go.

Done when a real Linux app opens out of its icon, appears as a card in the switcher, and closes back into the icon without a frame drop.

5

Wire up the transports

waypipe, Waydroid, Moonlight — each registered as an app with its own icon, its own switcher card, and its own connection state. Waydroid in multi-window mode gives each Android app its own Wayland surface, which is what makes them feel independent rather than mirrored.

Done when a home-node app, an Android app and a desktop stream sit side by side in the switcher and none of them looks like a remote session.

6

Boot into it

The Pi comes up straight into the shell with no desktop behind it, no cursor, no way out without a keyboard. Kiosk hardening, watchdog, auto-reconnect on the transports.

Done when you can pull the power, plug it back in, and it returns to the lock screen with its sessions restored.

The numbers

You asked for the layout exactly. This is what "exactly" means.

  • home grid4 × 6 = 24 per page, plus a 4-slot dock. Varies by device size and text size — make it config, never a constant.
  • column gapAt 393 pt wide with 60 pt icons and 27 pt margins: (393 − 240 − 54) ÷ 3 = 33 pt.
  • icon shapeA superellipse, not a rounded rect. Sample |cos t|^(2/n), |sin t|^(2/n) at n = 5 and use it as a mask so it scales.
  • rubber bandoffset = (x · d · c) / (d + c · |x|) — x past the edge, d the dimension, c = 0.55.
  • deceleration0.998 normal, 0.99 fast.
  • materialsBackdrop blur plus a saturation boost around 180%, with vibrancy on the text above it.
  • motionSprings, never bezier curves — and every one of them interruptible.

Motion is where the feel lives, not the pixels. Roughly 80% of "that's an iPhone" is spring physics and gesture-driven, grabbable transitions. If your animations are ease-in-out and cannot be caught mid-flight, it will never feel right no matter how accurate the layout is. Budget for this like it is the main work, because it is.

What to use rather than write

Tagged by how you should treat each one.

Framework7build on A web framework whose entire purpose is an iOS theme — navbars, sheets, list views, swipe-back. The fastest route through Phase 1.
Konsta UIbuild on Same author, Tailwind-based iOS and Material components. Pick this over Framework7 if you are already in Tailwind.
Flutter — Cupertino librarybuild on Google's open-source recreation of the iOS widget set. Runs on bare KMS/DRM via flutter-pi, no X or Wayland underneath.
Plasma Mobile · Lomiri · Phoshread first Three shipping mobile Linux shells. Do not fork them for the look — read how they handle window lifecycle and the app switcher, which is the part you would otherwise get wrong twice.
QtWaylandCompositorbuild on Lets your QML shell be the compositor. The single most important dependency in Phase 4.
Smithayonly if Rust compositor toolkit. Reach for it only if you have a reason to reject Qt — it is more control and considerably more work.
waypipebuild on Forwards individual Wayland apps over SSH. The reason home-node apps can feel independent instead of mirrored.
Waydroid · redroidbuild on Android in a container — locally on the Pi, or in the cloud with a WebRTC pipeline. Waydroid needs binder kernel modules; check your distro ships them before committing.
Sunshine + Moonlightbuild on Self-hosted, hardware-encoded desktop streaming with a good Pi client. For the heavy apps that per-window forwarding cannot carry.
Inter · Phosphor · Lucidebuild on Type and icons. Inter is deliberately close to SF's feel. Apple's SF Pro and SF Symbols are licensed and not redistributable — this is the substitution that keeps you clear.

What will actually bite you

In rough order of how much time it will cost.

  • The touchscreen, more than the software. A cheap digitiser with poor palm rejection and 80 ms of latency kills the illusion no matter how good your springs are. Spend real money here; it is the one component you cannot fix in code.
  • Live blur at 60 fps. Backdrop blur is the thing most likely to blow your frame budget on a Pi. Mitigate with pre-blurred wallpaper layers instead of a live filter. The prototype has a blur toggle and a frame counter under the bezel so you can measure this before you commit.
  • A Pi is the wrong shape for a phone. Battery, modem, suspend/resume and thermals are a separate multi-month project with none of the fun. As a wall panel, desk display or big-screen thin client — which is what you actually described — it is an excellent fit. Aim there.
  • Settings has no bottom. iOS Settings in full is essentially unbounded, and most of its panes have no Linux equivalent. The schema approach in Phase 3 is what keeps this finite.
  • Legal. Personal use is fine. Do not distribute or sell it, and ship none of Apple's fonts, symbols, icon artwork or wallpapers — redistribution is the line that gets enforced, not the resemblance.

Where the prototype gets you

Honest accounting of what is real in the linked build.

Real: the spring integrator, and every animation running through it with position and velocity handed back on interrupt. Rubber-banded pagination. Superellipse icon masks. Gesture-driven app open and close from the icon rect. Swipe-up-and-hold to the switcher, armed on dwell time rather than velocity. Control Center and Notification Center as draggable overlays. A schema-driven Settings app where Brightness dims the actual screen and Text Size re-flows the actual grid. A working calculator, notes, photos and player.

Not real: it is a web page, so there is no compositor and no transport. Every "app" is drawn by the same page rather than hosted. That is exactly the Phase 4 boundary — and the point of stopping there is that Phases 1 through 3 are worth validating before you spend a month on Wayland surface lifecycles.
